Evidence method

See what supports a result.

Our checks keep source evidence, supplied information, and product findings distinct. This page explains the method; the relevant regulator or authority remains the primary source for requirements.

A useful preflight should show what it checked, what is missing, and where a person needs to decide.

1. Identify the source

Regulatory workflows point to the public authority or official material that supports a reviewed rule.

2. Check the supplied facts

Industrial tools compare user-provided files and expectations against documented structural checks.

3. Bound the result

Findings remain tied to the checked source or file; uncertainty and review needs stay visible.

If information is missing

Do not guess. Share what you know and label the rest as unknown. The workflow should request only facts that could change the next step and keep the result incomplete until they are available.

Identify the gap

Separate required facts from optional context and explain why a missing fact matters.

Ask only what is needed

Request the smallest set of additional information that could change the result.

Pause when needed

Keep the result marked incomplete or for review until decisive evidence is available.

Industrial QA checks

Industrial QA tools compare files such as CSV exports, cable inventories, test limits, or OPC UA NodeSet XML with documented checks. They report what matched or changed in the supplied evidence. They do not establish manufacturer approval, standards certification, runtime commissioning, or product safety.

Independence and limits

RegEvidenceHub is privately operated and independent of regulators, councils, government departments, manufacturers, and standards bodies. It does not issue their decisions or approvals. Public availability of a source does not mean we own or republish it.
