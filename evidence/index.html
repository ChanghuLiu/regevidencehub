Evidence

Evidence is part of the product

RegEvidenceHub does not treat a regulatory webpage as a timeless rule. Decision-bearing sources are tracked separately from the rules derived from them.

When evidence changes, becomes unavailable, conflicts with another current source, or requires review, affected conclusions can be withheld instead of silently continuing with an outdated assumption.

Source provenance

Where the regulatory fact came from and which official source supports the reviewed rule.

Source status

Whether the reviewed evidence remains usable, changed, unavailable, conflicting, or pending review.

Decision status

Whether the available evidence and supplied facts are sufficient for the requested conclusion.

Fail-closed behavior

Affected conclusions are withheld or routed to review when required evidence cannot safely support them.

When some details are missing

Share the facts you know and label the rest as unknown. Do not fill gaps with guesses. Each product page lists the information and file types that its workflow supports.

1. Identify the gap

A useful preflight should distinguish information required to continue from optional context, and explain briefly why a required fact matters.

2. Ask only for what is needed

Request the smallest set of missing facts that could change the next step. Keep details already supplied, and avoid requesting sensitive information unless the workflow needs it.

3. Keep the result bounded

Until required facts are available, treat the result as incomplete or for review. Do not present a route or status as a confirmed decision, and do not imply that unknown facts were verified.

For supported inputs, scope, and the appropriate next step, open the relevant product page. Public AI-safe product information can link to these official product pages without inserting a purchase link into an AI tool response.

What we do not claim

Public availability of a source does not itself mean RegEvidenceHub owns or republishes that source content. The products are designed around structured facts, bounded evidence metadata, source links, and reviewed rule logic rather than wholesale republication of third-party pages or documents.
